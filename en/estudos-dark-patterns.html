Dark patterns and the design engineer

Subscribing takes two clicks, cancelling takes ten screens. An assignment on that dilemma, the solution I argued for and what the professor's feedback taught me.

full paper in pdf (portuguese) ↗︎

problem

Subscribing to a service takes two clicks, cancelling takes ten screens. Is that ethical?

my role

I chose the dilemma and built the analysis in five stages, using Kant and utilitarianism.

result

The feedback highlighted my clear thinking and academic writing.

The topic

Before college, this already annoyed me as a user: a subscription service signs you up in a few clicks, but to cancel you go through long, confusing screens hidden in menus nobody can find. Design has a name for this. It's a dark pattern, a term coined by Harry Brignull for interfaces built to lead users into a decision they wouldn't make if they saw everything clearly.

The Ethics course was built around a single assignment: choose a dilemma from your own profession and argue for a fair solution, one stage per week. I chose this one.

How the assignment was built

  1. 01

    Propose the dilemma week 2

    I chose hidden cancellation in subscription apps. It already annoyed me as a user, and one day I could be the person who builds that screen, or refuses to.

  2. 02

    Is there a right answer? week 3

    Both sides are legitimate. The company needs to retain customers, and that keeps people employed. Users have the right to decide about their own money without barriers invented by design.

  3. 03

    Separate morality, law and ethics week 4

    Morality is what the group accepts, law is what the state imposes, ethics asks whether it holds up. In the industry, making cancellation hard is called a "retention best practice": it's legal, it's accepted, and it still fails the test.

  4. 04

    Build the argument week 5

    Kant: the user is an end, not a means to hit a retention target. Utilitarianism: the short-term gain turns into lost trust, brand damage and regulatory pressure. Both arrive at the same place by different paths.

  5. 05

    Propose a fair solution week 6

    Transparency by default. The same ease the company offers to generate revenue has to exist for the user to leave.

Cancelling a subscription should be as simple and accessible as signing up for it.

Who loses the most

People less familiar with technology, like older adults. Some are paying for services they no longer use just because they couldn't find the cancel button. And the designer who agrees to build that screen, even under pressure, helps normalize manipulation as a professional practice.

Professor's feedback
